How your circle is judged

  1. Draw one loop in a single stroke, anywhere and any size. Nothing is marked out in advance: there is no target circle and no fixed center.
  2. Locate the center. When you lift, the app works out where the center of your drawing is. It is the point whose distance to every part of your stroke is as even as possible (a least-squares best-fit circle, the usual reference for measuring roundness).
  3. Average distance. Your radius is the average distance from that center to your stroke. The dashed circle shows it.
  4. Score (% mode). The app measures how far your stroke strays from that average, as a root-mean-square percentage of the radius. Every 1% of wobble costs 5 points, and leaving a gap in the loop costs about 3 points per 10°. Going a little past the start is fine.

Two ways to score

% (classic): 100 minus 5 points per 1% of wobble, minus the gap penalty. Easy to read, but the 5 and the penalty are hand-picked.

bits (precision): p = ½·log2(1 + r̄² / σ²) ≈ log2(r̄ / σ). Here r̄ is the average radius and σ is the root-mean-square distance between your stroke and that circle, measured both ways (stroke to circle and circle to stroke), so a gap counts as error by itself. There are no tuning constants. Each extra bit means your wobble halved, and “1 part in N” means σ is 1/N of the radius. The score has no ceiling: it climbs faster and faster, with infinite slope, as the circle approaches perfect. It is what an extreme exponent like 1 − (σ/r̄)1/1000 becomes once it is rescaled by 1000.

Your stroke is colored by how far each part is from the average radius. The ring marks the biggest miss. The stroke is resampled evenly along its length, so drawing faster or slower in places doesn’t skew the result.

Trace practice

Tap Trace to practise on a guide circle. Set its radius with the slider, or tap Random size, then trace over it in one stroke. Tick Hide guide while drawing to draw it from memory. A trace is judged against the guide itself: the same % and bits formulas, with the guide’s center and radius as the reference, so being too big, too small or off-center all count. The card also shows how round your shape was on its own (Shape alone) and how far its located center landed from the guide’s. Traces are practice only and aren’t saved to your stats; the card keeps a tally for the session.

Stats: every closed circle, where the end of your stroke meets or crosses the start, is saved on this device. Open Stats to see your averages, trends and history, or to export them. Circles with a gap are still scored, but not saved.

iPad: once you draw with Apple Pencil, touches from your hand are ignored. Tap the Pencil only chip to draw with a finger again. Laptop: click and drag with a mouse or trackpad. Press Esc to clear.
